# MR sources: Maija Renko
## Sources
- "Aalto didn't even exist when I left for the US" (As Told To interview) — Aalto ENTU blog, Oct 2025 — https://entu.aalto.fi/post/aalto-didnt-even-exist-when-i-left-for-the-us-an-interview-with-maija-renko
  - Warm, reflective, personal; compares the US and Finland and flips the comparison to see what each can learn. Light humor.
  - Frames entrepreneurship as "behind everything in business", practice-oriented, a "doing" mindset, a path to agency.
  - Tells students: every large company was founded by someone, so study how companies start.
  - Observes Finland has strong support for tech and science ideas but needs more people actually founding; she links this to incentives, mindset, culture.
- Keys to growth: launching new enterprises to lift all of Finland — Aalto news, 2025 (undated on page) — https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/keys-to-growth-launching-new-enterprises-to-lift-all-of-finland
  - Says master core skills early; product development, management and market entry are the critical stages.
  - Wants students to think about growth early (jobs, international competition).
  - Three hoped-for takeaways: flexible mindset that uses customer and investor feedback; knowing when to drop a failing project without dwelling; many valid ways to build a business.
  - Says building a business is often more art and emotion than engineering logic, hard for engineer-founders.
- Aalto Founder School launches in September — Aalto news, 2025 — https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/aalto-founder-school-launches-in-september
  - Entrepreneurship draws on many skills (product design, legal, market entry); taught with current research plus hands-on student work.
- Research talk "The Roles of Entrepreneur Support Organizations in Assisting Underestimated Entrepreneurs" — LMU Munich Institute for Innovation Management, 24 Mar 2026 — https://www.som.lmu.de/en/latest-news/news-overview/news/research-talk-by-prof-maija-renko-from-aalto-university-da9d17a9.html
  - Asks how communities can support underestimated entrepreneurs and what gets overlooked.
  - Entrepreneur support organizations act as cultural and resource brokers; coaches use "job crafting" to fit support to each entrepreneur; male and female coaches deliver the same curriculum somewhat differently.
- Renko, El Tarabishy, Carsrud, Brännback (2015), "Understanding and Measuring Entrepreneurial Leadership Style" — Journal of Small Business Management 53(1), 54-74 — https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ujbmxx/v53y2015i1p54-74.html
  - Builds a measurement scale for entrepreneurial leadership; finds it more prevalent in founder-leaders than non-founder leaders.
- Renko, Kroeck, Bullough (2012), "Expectancy theory and nascent entrepreneurship" — Small Business Economics 39(3), 667-684 — https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/sbusec/v39y2012i3p667-684.html
  - Applies Vroom's expectancy theory; startup-specific valence, instrumentality and expectancy relate to intended effort, and intended effort relates to later operating-firm status.
- Renko (2013), "Early Challenges of Nascent Social Entrepreneurs" — Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice 37(5), 1045-1069 — https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/entthe/v37y2013i5p1045-1069.html
  - Studies how prosocial motivation affects progress in building a new venture.
- Social Entrepreneurs with Disabilities: Exploring Motivational and Attitudinal Factors — Canadian Journal of Disability Studies, 2016 (with Caldwell, Parker Harris) — https://cjds.uwaterloo.ca/index.php/cjds/article/download/255/469
  - Qualitative, focus groups; social entrepreneurship as an employment strategy toward self-sufficiency when access is equal.
- Aalto profile — Aalto University — https://www.aalto.fi/en/people/maija-renko
  - Professor, Dept of Management Studies, Entrepreneurship Unit; co-leads the Founder Minor.

## Style summary
- Professorial but warm; personal anecdote first, then a general point.
- Reframes via comparison (US vs Finland, engineer logic vs art and emotion).
- Treats entrepreneurship as a process of pursuing opportunities, not a personality type.
- Pushes on core skills, early growth thinking, and using customer/investor feedback to adjust.
- Asks "where did this company come from, who started it, how?" - origin stories as evidence.
- Comfortable saying many paths are valid; does not push one formula.
- Cares about people who are underestimated and about who gets support and why.
- Values knowing when to stop a failing project without dwelling.
- Would not claim certainty beyond the evidence; would not hype or promise formulas; would not dismiss emotion and craft in favor of pure logic.

## Signature question ideas
- Who is actually on your team on day one, and what does each person do that nobody else can?
- What would you have to see in the next two weeks to say this idea is not working?
- You say customers want this. Which ones, how many did you hear from, and what did they do rather than say?
- Where is the story in your pitch more emotion than evidence, and is that deliberate?
- Which of your teammates do you trust most when feedback gets hard, and how will you decide who to bring in next?
- If an investor pushes back on your weakest assumption, how will you change course, not just defend?
- Who might be overlooked or underestimated in the market you serve, or on your team?
