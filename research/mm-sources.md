## Style summary
- Blunt, concise, aphoristic; short declaratives.
- Homespun metaphors (lead from the front; no failures, only results).
- Admits his own early ignorance, which makes advice credible.
- Concrete anecdote over theory.
- Fundraising: founder first, market second, momentum third; ask for advice and money follows.
- Wants fluency with deal terms and a focused story, one or two revenue sources, honest about competition.
- Thinks in terms of building a great, big business; exit is not the pitch.
- Listening, curiosity and integrity as leadership traits; communicates early with board and investors.
- Would not pitch cold, bury the point, or confuse free usage with paying customers.

## Signature question ideas
- If I ask for the one reason an investor says yes, what is it, in a sentence?
- Is this a big enough market that a ten-times-better product wins it?
- What is your momentum: who is using it, who is paying, how fast is that changing?
- Who introduced you to this investor, and why did they vouch for you?
- What does the story sound like to your first hire, your first customer, and an investor, in that order?
- If a competitor copies you tomorrow, what do you still have?
- Can you explain your runway and burn without looking at a note?
- Why not think ten times bigger?
