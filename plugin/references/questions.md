# Questions

Starting points, not a script. Ask one per turn, built on the sprinter's last answer and put in your persona's voice. Never read a section out as a list. Openers and angles come first; the later sections are for when a specific gap or number needs it.

## Openers
One provocative, person-level question to start. Pick the one that fits the person and where they are in the sprint.
- What's the most interesting thing about you that isn't in your pitch?
- What do people usually get wrong about you, or about what you're building?
- When did you last surprise yourself during this sprint?
- What do you know about this problem that most people in the demo-day room don't?
- What's a moment from the sprint you tell friends but have never put in the pitch?
- What do you believe about this market that most people disagree with?
- What part of this would you keep building even if nobody showed up on demo day?
- What almost made you stop, and why didn't you?
- A week after demo day, what do you want the room still thinking about you?
- What's the strangest thing that happened on the way to this idea?

## Angles
When a story stalls, offer one new way in instead of another question.
- Tell it the way you would to a friend at 2 am.
- Tell the version where it went wrong.
- Answer the sceptic in the room first.
- Tell it from the customer's side.
- Start at demo day, then go back to where it began.

## Capture — origin, audience, moments
For a moment that has no scene yet: where it happened, or who was there.
- Where were you when that happened, and who else was in the room?
- Who was the first person who really needed what you built? What happened with them?
- Who'll be in the room on demo day, and what do they already believe about this?

## Content: action, result, stakes
For a moment with no turn or result yet.
- What did you decide or do there, as distinct from the team?
- What was different afterwards, and how did you know?
- What would have been lost if that had gone the other way?

## Content: pruning and the point
For a moment that has no point yet.
- If the room remembers one thing from that moment, what should it be?
- If someone asked "so what?", what would you say in one sentence?
- What could you leave out and still have the story work?

## Recall: checking a number
For two different numbers for the same thing. Give both and where each came from, in their words. Ask once.
- On Tuesday you wrote "[their words]", and today you said "[their words]". How do those fit together?
- What was being counted each time?
- What would the number be if someone checked it today?

## Recall: from a document to a moment
For something that's only in a document they gave you. Name the line, not the document's author.
- Your deck says "[their line]". What was happening the day that became true?
- What does that line leave out that you remember?

## Recall: connecting moments
For a companion's reading, or a moment the story hasn't used yet. Offer the reading as a question, and drop it if it doesn't fit.
- "[Moment A]" and "[moment B]" both seem to turn on [a pattern]. Does that fit, or is something else going on?
- Which of the moments you've told me hasn't made it into the story yet? What would it show that the others don't?
- If I've read the thread between them wrong, what's actually connecting them?
